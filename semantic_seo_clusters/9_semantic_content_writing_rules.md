# 9. Semantic Content Writing Rules — How to Write Like a Pro

Welcome to Chapter 9! This is the LONGEST and most important chapter because it contains the actual RULES you must follow when writing content for Google. Think of these as the exam rules — if you break them, you fail. If you follow them, you get first position!

---

## RULE 1: Find & Use Entities, Attributes & Popular N-Grams in Article
### What is the rule?
Before writing, RESEARCH your topic. Find:
- **Entities:** The people, places, things related to your topic.
- **Attributes:** The facts/details about those entities.
- **Popular N-Grams:** The word groups (Bigrams, Trigrams) that top-ranking competitors use.

Then make sure ALL of them appear in your article.

### How to follow the rule:
1. Google your keyword. Open the top 5 results.
2. List every Entity mentioned (people, brands, locations).
3. List every Attribute mentioned (numbers, dates, facts).
4. Run an N-Gram analysis on the competitors.
5. Include all of them in your own article.

---

## RULE 2: Single Macro Context for Pages
### What is the rule?
Every page must have ONE and ONLY ONE big topic (Macro Context). Do not mix multiple unrelated topics on one page.

- ✅ A page about "Chicken Biryani Recipe" → Only talks about Biryani.
- ❌ A page about "Biryani Recipe + How to Fix Your WiFi" → Two totally unrelated topics!

### How to follow the rule:
- Before publishing, ask: "If I read ONLY the headings of this page, is there ONE clear topic?" If the headings jump between unrelated subjects, split them into separate pages.

---

## RULE 3: Why Use Factual Sentences
### What is the rule?
Google's NLP loves FACTS. A factual sentence contains provable data — numbers, dates, measurements, names. Google can verify these against its Knowledge Base.

- ❌ "Mangoes are really tasty." (Opinion — cannot be verified.)
- ✅ "Mangoes contain 60 calories per 100 grams and are rich in Vitamin C." (Fact — can be verified.)

### How to follow the rule:
- Replace EVERY opinion sentence with a factual sentence. If you can't find a fact, research more.

---

## RULE 4: Using Research Papers to Prove Points
### What is the rule?
When making a strong claim, back it up with a credible source. Research papers, government statistics, and official reports are the gold standard.

### How to follow the rule:
- Link to the source. "According to the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics (2023), the literacy rate is 62.3%."
- Use Google Scholar (scholar.google.com) to find free research papers on your topic.

---

## RULE 5: Don't Break the Context
### What is the rule?
Once you start talking about a topic in a section, do NOT suddenly jump to a completely different topic. Finish the current topic first, then start a new section.

- ❌ "Biryani uses Basmati rice. Speaking of rice, did you know that Japan produces sushi rice? Anyway, back to Biryani masala..."
- ✅ "Biryani uses Basmati rice. The best Basmati rice is long-grain and aromatic, typically grown in Punjab."

### How to follow the rule:
- Read each paragraph. Does every sentence belong to the same topic? If one sentence wanders off, move it to the correct section.

---

## RULE 6: Optimize Discourse Integration
### What is the rule?
**Discourse Integration** means every sentence should logically connect to the next. There should be a smooth "flow" from one idea to another.

- ❌ "Biryani is popular. The moon is far away. Chicken is a good protein." (No flow!)
- ✅ "Biryani is popular across Pakistan. One reason for its popularity is the use of tender chicken, which provides excellent protein along with rich flavor." (Smooth flow!)

### How to follow the rule:
- Use connecting words: "Therefore," "As a result," "Building on this," "Additionally," "In contrast."

---

## RULE 7: Don't Delay the Answer
### What is the rule?
If a user searches for "What is the capital of Pakistan?" and clicks on your page, they want the answer IMMEDIATELY. Do NOT write a 3-paragraph introduction about the history of Pakistan before finally saying "Islamabad."

### How to follow the rule:
- **Answer in the first sentence after the heading.**
  - H2: "What is the capital of Pakistan?"
  - First sentence: "The capital of Pakistan is Islamabad."
  - Then you can add more context below.

---

## RULE 8: Expand the Evidence with Variations
### What is the rule?
Don't just state a fact once. Support it with different types of evidence: examples, comparisons, statistics, and expert quotes.

### How to follow the rule:
- State the fact: "Electric cars are becoming popular."
- Add a statistic: "Sales grew by 35% in 2023."
- Add an example: "For instance, the Tesla Model 3 is now the best-selling car in Norway."
- Add a comparison: "Compared to 2020, the number of charging stations has doubled."

---

## RULE 9: Don't Break the Information Graph
### What is the rule?
The **Information Graph** is the network of connected facts on your page. If you state a fact in one section, don't contradict it in another section!

- ❌ Section 1: "Lahore's population is 13 million." Section 5: "Lahore, a city of 15 million people."
- ✅ Use the same number consistently throughout.

### How to follow the rule:
- Keep a fact sheet for your article. Every number, date, and name should be consistent everywhere.

---

## RULE 10: Use Shorter Sentences
### What is the rule?
Long sentences are hard for BOTH humans and Google's NLP to understand. Keep sentences under 20 words whenever possible.

- ❌ "Biryani, which is a very popular and delicious rice dish that many people across Pakistan and India enjoy eating, especially during festivals and weddings, is typically made with chicken or mutton." (37 words — too long!)
- ✅ "Biryani is a popular rice dish in Pakistan and India. It is typically made with chicken or mutton. People often serve it during festivals and weddings." (3 short sentences, much clearer!)

---

## RULE 11: Decrease Count of Contextless Words
### What is the rule?
**Contextless words** are words that don't add any meaning: "very," "really," "quite," "basically," "actually," "just," "stuff," "things."

These words waste space and reduce Information Density.

- ❌ "It's actually really quite a very good phone." (7 words, zero information!)
- ✅ "It's an excellent phone." (4 words, clear message!)

---

## RULE 12: More Information Per Page, Section, Paragraph
### What is the rule?
Every section and paragraph should be PACKED with useful information. No filler. No fluff. No repeating the same point in different words.

### How to follow the rule:
- After writing each paragraph, count the facts in it. If it has zero facts, rewrite it. If it has 1 fact, add 2 more.

---

## RULE 13: Consistent Style Across Documents
### What is the rule?
All articles on your website should follow the same writing style: same tone, same formatting, same heading structure. Consistency = professionalism = trust.

### How to follow the rule:
- Create a Style Guide for your website. Define: heading format, paragraph length, bullet point style, image placement, and tone of voice.

---

## RULE 14: Consistent Declarations
### What is the rule?
If you call something by one name, ALWAYS use that name. Don't switch between different labels for the same thing.

- ❌ "SEO, Search Engine Optimization, Search Marketing, Web Ranking" (all used interchangeably).
- ✅ Introduce it once: "Search Engine Optimization (SEO)." Then always use "SEO."

---

## RULE 15: Unique N-Grams
### What is the rule?
Include word groups that competitors DON'T have. These prove your content offers new information. (See Chapter 8 for details.)

---

## RULE 16: Same N-Grams
### What is the rule?
Also include the word groups that ALL competitors DO have. Missing expected N-Grams makes your content look incomplete.

---

## RULE 17: Single Topic with Every Detail
### What is the rule?
Cover ONE topic but cover it with EVERY possible detail. Go ultra-deep. If your topic is "Chicken Biryani," cover: history, regional variations, ingredients, step-by-step recipe, tips, common mistakes, nutritional info, storage, reheating, and FAQs.

---

## RULE 18: Fewer Links
### What is the rule?
Don't overload your article with links. Each link is a signal to Google, and too many links dilute the power of each one.

- A 1,500-word article should have 5-10 internal links and 2-3 external links maximum.

---

## RULE 19: Use Ordered and Unordered Lists
### What is the rule?
Google LOVES lists. They are easy to scan, easy to extract for Featured Snippets, and have high Information Density.

- Use **Numbered lists** for steps, rankings, or sequences (1, 2, 3).
- Use **Bullet points** for groups of items with no particular order.

---

## RULE 20: How to Give Long Form Answers
### What is the rule?
For complex questions, structure your answer with:
1. A short summary (2-3 sentences).
2. A detailed explanation broken into sub-sections.
3. Examples for each point.
4. A conclusion.

---

## RULE 21: Key Term in Title and Heading
### What is the rule?
Your main keyword MUST appear in:
- The Title Tag
- The H1 heading
- At least one H2 heading
- The first 100 words of the article

---

## RULE 22: 40 Words for Featured Snippet
### What is the rule?
Google's Featured Snippet (the answer box at the top of search results) is typically 40-50 words long. Write your answer passage to be exactly 40-50 words.

### How to follow the rule:
- Count the words! After your question-heading, write a self-contained answer paragraph of exactly 40-50 words.

---

## RULE 23: Never Give Opinion in an Article
### What is the rule?
Google's NLP cannot verify opinions. Stick to facts.

- ❌ "I think this is the best phone ever made."
- ✅ "This phone received a 4.8/5 rating from over 10,000 users on Amazon."

---

## RULE 24: Never Use Everyday Language in Article
### What is the rule?
Avoid casual, informal language in articles. Use professional vocabulary.

- ❌ "This thing is super cool and like, really awesome."
- ✅ "This product features innovative design and high-performance specifications."

---

## RULE 25: Don't Use Analogies
### What is the rule?
Analogies ("The car flies like a cheetah") confuse NLP models. They are figurative language, and Google takes words literally.

- ❌ "The processor is a beast." (Google thinks there's an actual animal.)
- ✅ "The processor handles 1 billion calculations per second." (Clear, factual.)

**Note:** This is a rule for SEO content specifically. In creative writing, analogies are fine.

---

## RULE 26: Citing Authoritative Sources
### What is the rule?
When you state a fact, link to the official source. This builds trust (E-E-A-T).

- "According to WHO, approximately 1.35 million people die each year in road traffic crashes." [Link to WHO]

---

## RULE 27: Content Length Rules
### What is the rule?
- **Short-form content:** 500-800 words for simple factual queries.
- **Medium-form content:** 1,500-2,500 words for detailed how-to guides.
- **Long-form content:** 3,000-5,000+ words for comprehensive pillar articles.

Match the length to the query complexity. A simple question doesn't need 5,000 words. A complex topic shouldn't be crammed into 500 words.

---

## RULE 28: Don't Create Extra Sentences Without Logical Reason
### What is the rule?
Every sentence must earn its place. If a sentence doesn't add NEW information, DELETE it.

- ❌ "This is very important to note. It should be noted that. Keep in mind that." (3 sentences saying nothing!)

---

## RULE 29: Adding Perspective Richness
### What is the rule?
Under each heading, provide multiple perspectives: historical, scientific, practical, economic, and user experience perspectives.

### How to follow the rule:
- H2: "Benefits of Electric Cars"
  - Environmental perspective: Lower emissions.
  - Economic perspective: Lower fuel costs.
  - Practical perspective: Fewer maintenance visits.
  - User perspective: Quieter driving experience.

---

## RULE 30: Why No Question Copy Pasting
### What is the rule?
Don't just copy questions from "People Also Ask" and paste them as headings without tailoring them. Rewrite them naturally to match your article's flow.

---

## RULE 31: Why No Linking to Citations While Writing Content
### What is the rule?
Don't interrupt your writing flow to insert citation links mid-sentence. Instead, add citations at the END of the paragraph or in a references section.

---

## RULE 32: Table Context
### What is the rule?
When using tables, always add a context sentence BEFORE and AFTER the table explaining what it shows and what the reader should take away from it.

---

## RULE 33: Why Use Abbreviation
### What is the rule?
Use abbreviations to increase Information Density. But ALWAYS spell out the full term first.

- ✅ "Natural Language Processing (NLP) is used by search engines. NLP helps Google understand..." 
- ❌ "NLP is used by search engines." (Reader doesn't know what NLP means!)

---

## RULE 34: Don't Puzzle Reader or Google Bot
### What is the rule?
Avoid phrases like "as mentioned above," "go back to section 2," or "see the previous chart." Google's bot reads linearly and doesn't "go back."

### How to follow the rule:
- Repeat the key information briefly instead of referencing another section. Each section should be self-contained.

---

## RULE 35: Give Safe Answer
### What is the rule?
When the answer is debatable, present ALL sides and cite evidence for each. Don't pick a side without evidence.

- ✅ "Laser cutting is considered safe when proper safety equipment is used. However, studies show that without ventilation, fumes can cause respiratory issues (Source: OSHA)."

---

## RULE 36: Signal the Answer Part
### What is the rule?
Tell Google which part of your text IS the answer. Use signal phrases.

- ✅ "**The answer is...**" / "**The main reason is...**" / "**In short,...**"
- Bold the answer sentence so Google's IE (Information Extraction) can easily grab it.

---

## RULE 37: Why Put "If" in Second Statement
### What is the rule?
In semantic writing, state the main fact FIRST, then add the condition.

- ❌ "If you want to lose weight, you should exercise daily." (Condition first, answer second.)
- ✅ "Exercising daily helps with weight loss, especially if combined with a balanced diet." (Answer first, condition second.)

---

## RULE 38: Optimize Subordinate Text First Sentence
### What is the rule?
Start subordinate clauses with "To" + action verb. This creates a clear purpose statement.

- ✅ "**To improve your website speed,** compress all images before uploading."
- ✅ "**To build topical authority,** publish 2 related articles per week."

---

## RULE 39: Give Examples After a Plural Noun
### What is the rule?
Whenever you use a plural noun (fruits, languages, cities), IMMEDIATELY give examples.

- ❌ "There are many fruits in Pakistan." (Which fruits? The reader doesn't know!)
- ✅ "There are many fruits in Pakistan, such as mangoes, oranges, guavas, and pomegranates."

---

## RULE 40: Choose Predicates Wisely
### What is the rule?
A **predicate** is the verb in your sentence. Different verbs have different semantic weights.

- "Temperature **increases**" (neutral, factual)
- "Temperature **surges**" (implies sudden, dramatic change)
- "Temperature **improves**" (implies it was bad before)

Choose the verb that most accurately describes the action.

### How to follow the rule:
- Read your sentence. Is the verb the best possible choice? "The company **made** a phone" → "The company **manufactured** a smartphone." More specific = higher semantic value.

---

## RULE 41: Be Specific When Describing Things
### What is the rule?
Move from generic to specific. Don't be vague.

- ❌ "This phone has good battery life."
- ✅ "This phone has a 5,000 mAh battery that lasts approximately 14 hours on a single charge under normal usage."

---

## RULE 42: Use Numeric Values
### What is the rule?
Replace vague words with exact numbers whenever possible.

- ❌ "A lot of people use smartphones."
- ✅ "Approximately 6.8 billion people use smartphones worldwide as of 2024."

---

## RULE 43: Cut the Fluff Out
### What is the rule?
Remove all unnecessary words. If a sentence works without a word, that word is fluff.

- ❌ "Basically, in order to actually improve your SEO, you really need to definitely focus on quality."
- ✅ "To improve your SEO, focus on quality."

---

## RULE 44: Be Certain
### What is the rule?
Avoid wishy-washy, uncertain language. Google trusts confident, factual statements.

- ❌ "It might be possible that exercise could potentially help with weight loss."
- ✅ "Regular exercise helps with weight loss."

---

## RULE 45: Same Part of Speech Tag in Listings
### What is the rule?
When making a bullet list, start every item with the same type of word (all verbs, all nouns, or all adjectives).

- ❌ Mixed list:
  - Running is good (verb)
  - Healthy food (adjective)
  - To sleep early (infinitive)

- ✅ Consistent list (all verbs):
  - Run for 30 minutes daily
  - Eat healthy food
  - Sleep early every night

---

## RULE 46: Context in Sentences
### What is the rule?
Every sentence should contain context that helps Google disambiguate the meaning.

- ❌ "Mercury is dangerous." (The planet? The element? The car brand?)
- ✅ "Mercury, a toxic heavy metal with the chemical symbol Hg, is dangerous." (Context = chemical, element, Hg.)

---

## RULE 47: Prioritize Attributes and Contexts
### What is the rule?
When answering a question, put the most important attribute FIRST, followed by supporting context.

- ✅ "Lahore's population is 13 million (as of 2024 census), making it the second-largest city in Pakistan."
- ❌ "Lahore is a historic city in Punjab with many monuments, and its population is around 13 million."

---

## RULE 48: Why Use Diverse Measurement Units
### What is the rule?
Different users understand different units. Include multiple units.

- ✅ "The tower is 330 meters (1,083 feet) tall and weighs approximately 10,100 tonnes (22.2 million pounds)."
- This improves semantic relevance because users might search in meters OR feet.

---

## RULE 49: How to Answer Boolean Questions
### What is the rule?
A **Boolean Question** is a Yes/No question. Answer with "Yes" or "No" FIRST, then explain.

- Question: "Can you drink sea water?"
- ✅ "No, you cannot drink sea water. It contains approximately 35 grams of salt per liter, which can cause dehydration and kidney damage."

---

## RULE 50: Grammar and Spelling Mistakes
### What is the rule?
Grammar and spelling errors damage your E-E-A-T score. Google's Quality Raters consider sloppy writing as a sign of low quality.

### How to follow the rule:
- Use tools: Grammarly, Hemingway Editor, or Screaming Frog (to find errors at scale).
- Read your article out loud before publishing. You'll catch awkward phrases.

---

## RULE 51: Truth Ranges
### What is the rule?
When sources disagree on a number, present a **Truth Range** — the range of all reported values.

- ❌ "The population of Lahore is 13 million." (One source says 11 million, another says 15 million.)
- ✅ "The population of Lahore is estimated between 11 and 15 million, depending on the source and methodology used."

---

## RULE 52: How to Optimize Content for NLP
### What is the rule?
Write in a way that makes NLP models' job easy:
1. Use Subject-Verb-Object sentences.
2. Use active voice (not passive).
3. Define terms before using them.
4. Use clear pronoun references.
5. Keep sentences under 20 words.

---

## RULE 53: Relevance Configuration
### What is the rule?
**Relevance Configuration** means rearranging your sentence so the answer comes FIRST. This improves "micro semantics."

- ❌ "Because of various factors including climate, soil quality, and irrigation, Punjab produces 80% of Pakistan's wheat."
- ✅ "Punjab produces 80% of Pakistan's wheat due to favorable climate, soil quality, and irrigation."

---

## RULE 54: How to Answer Long Form Type Questions
### What is the rule?
For long-form listing questions (e.g., "What are the types of...?"), use this structure:
1. Short summary sentence.
2. Numbered list of types.
3. Brief explanation for each type.
4. Source citation.

---

## RULE 55: Why TOC is Important and Major Ranking Factor
### What is the rule?
A **Table of Contents (TOC)** at the top of every long article is a MAJOR ranking factor.

Why?
- It shows Google the Contextual Flow of your article.
- Users can jump to the section they need (reducing bounce rate).
- Google uses the TOC to understand your heading hierarchy.

### How to follow the rule:
- Add a clickable TOC after your introduction, BEFORE the first H2 section.

---

## RULE 56: Rare Entities & Attributes
### What is the rule?
Including **rare entities and attributes** that competitors missed gives you a huge Information Gain Score boost.

- If every competitor mentions "Babar Azam's batting average," but nobody mentions "Babar Azam's strike rate in the powerplay overs in Asia Cup 2023," that's a rare attribute worth including.

---

## RULE 57: What is Modality and How to Use It?
### What is the rule?
**Modality** is how certain or uncertain a statement is, expressed through modal verbs:
- **Certainty:** "Water boils at 100°C." (No modal verb — absolute fact.)
- **Possibility:** "This **could** cause issues." (Modal: could.)
- **Obligation:** "You **must** update your plugins." (Modal: must.)
- **Permission:** "You **can** use either method." (Modal: can.)

### How to follow the rule:
- Use "is" and "does" for confirmed facts.
- Use "can," "may," "might" only for possibilities.
- Use "must," "should" for recommendations.
- AVOID: "could potentially possibly maybe..." (too uncertain!).

---

## RULE 58: What is Coreference Error and How to Fix It?
### What is the rule?
A **Coreference Error** happens when a pronoun ("he," "she," "it," "they") is ambiguous.

- ❌ "Babar Azam bowled to Virat Kohli. **He** hit a six." (Who hit the six? Babar or Virat? Google doesn't know!)
- ✅ "Babar Azam bowled to Virat Kohli. **Kohli** hit a six." (Clear!)

### How to follow the rule:
- Use the entity's actual name instead of a pronoun whenever there could be confusion.

---

## RULE 59: What is Heartfelt SEO?
### What is the rule?
**Heartfelt SEO** means writing with empathy and a positive, warm tone. Avoid content that is:
- Scary or fear-mongering.
- Negative or discouraging.
- Cold and robotic.

Google's Quality Raters check the sentiment and tone of your content. A helpful, warm tone builds trust.

### How to follow the rule:
- Write as if you're helping a friend. "Don't worry, this is easier than it looks. Let's walk through it step by step."

---

## RULE 60: Focus on Entities Not Keywords
### What is the rule?
Stop thinking in "keywords." Start thinking in "entities."

- **Old way (keywords):** "Best cheap running shoes buy online Lahore Pakistan."
- **New way (entities):** Write about the Entity (running shoes), its Attributes (price, brand, comfort), and its connections to other entities (Nike, Adidas, jogging, marathons).

---

## RULE 61: Content Writing Rules (Summary)
### What is the rule?
A collection of micro-rules:
- Never start a sentence with "If" — put the statement first, condition second.
- Use abbreviations after defining them.
- Keep sentences short.
- Write small, one-token statements when listing facts.

---

## RULE 62: Improve Your Article Vocabulary
### What is the rule?
Use unique, domain-specific vocabulary. Google measures the richness of your vocabulary.

- ❌ Generic: "The car goes fast."
- ✅ Rich: "The sedan accelerates from 0 to 100 km/h in 5.2 seconds, powered by a turbocharged 2.0-liter engine."

### How to follow the rule:
- Use a thesaurus to find better words.
- Study expert articles in your field and note the vocabulary they use.
- Include technical terms (lemmas) that are specific to your domain.

---

## RULE 63: Content Originality
### What is the rule?
Originality is NOT just "not plagiarizing." True originality means:
- Providing a unique perspective.
- Adding original data or research.
- Sharing personal experience.
- Offering insights that competitors don't have.

Simply rephrasing a competitor's article is FAKE originality. Google can detect this.

---

## RULE 64: Entity Stuffing
### What is the rule?
Just like keyword stuffing is bad, **Entity Stuffing** is also bad. Don't mention an entity excessively just to look relevant.

- ❌ "Samsung Galaxy S24 is great. Samsung Galaxy S24 has a good camera. Samsung Galaxy S24 battery is strong. Samsung Galaxy S24 is the best Samsung Galaxy S24."
- ✅ Mention the entity naturally. Use pronouns ("it"), abbreviations ("the S24"), or descriptive phrases ("the device," "this smartphone") to vary your language.

### How to follow the rule:
- Filter attributes by **prominence, relevance, and popularity.** Only include the most important attributes of your Central Entity. Don't list 50 irrelevant attributes just to mention the entity more.

---

## RULE 65: Don't Create Extra Sentences
### What is the rule?
If a fact can be stated in ONE token/sentence, don't stretch it into 3 sentences.

- ❌ "There are many cities in Pakistan. These cities are located in different provinces. Some of these cities are very large."
- ✅ "Pakistan has several large cities, including Karachi, Lahore, and Islamabad, spread across its four provinces."

---

## RULE 66: Long Form vs Short Form
### What is the rule?
- **Short-form questions** (like "What is SEO?") should have short answers (40-60 words) → Google prefers these for Featured Snippets.
- **Long-form questions** (like "How does Google's ranking algorithm work?") should have long, detailed answers (500+ words).

### How to follow the rule:
- For each question-heading in your article, decide: Is this a short-form or long-form question? Match the answer length accordingly.
